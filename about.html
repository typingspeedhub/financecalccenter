About FinanceCalcCenter

FinanceCalcCenter is an independent educational website created by Tibor Győry. The site provides free financial calculators and practical money guides designed to make everyday financial calculations easier to understand.

Who Is Behind FinanceCalcCenter?

My name is Tibor Győry, and I am the founder and creator of FinanceCalcCenter. I have a degree in Business Informatics, which combines economic and financial subjects with information technology and data-based problem solving.

My interest in personal finance started long before this website existed. As a child, I watched my father carefully record household expenses in a squared notebook. That simple habit of knowing where the money goes stayed with me.

For approximately 30 years, I have kept track of my own income, expenses, and financial balance. Over time, the notebook was replaced by Excel spreadsheets, but the principle remained the same: understand the numbers, keep track of spending, and plan ahead whenever possible.

My Practical Experience With Personal Finance

Financial planning is part of my everyday life rather than just a subject I write about. I regularly set money aside for unexpected expenses, future goals, and retirement.

I believe in maintaining an emergency reserve and planning larger expenses in advance whenever possible. I generally avoid borrowing for everyday consumption and view long-term borrowing primarily as something that may be appropriate for major purposes such as purchasing a home.

Tracking income and expenses over many years has shown me how even relatively small recurring costs can affect a household budget over time. This practical perspective is one of the reasons I created calculators for savings, loans, recurring expenses, inflation, and other everyday financial decisions.

Continuous Learning

My interest in personal finance has also led me to continue learning about the subject independently. Over the years, I have read financial books and followed educational videos and other resources covering topics such as budgeting, saving, investing, debt, and long-term financial planning.

I do not present myself as a certified financial adviser. My background is in Business Informatics, combined with decades of personal experience in budgeting, tracking expenses, saving, and financial planning.

FinanceCalcCenter reflects that combination of practical experience, economic thinking, and an interest in using calculations and technology to make financial concepts easier to understand.

Why I Created FinanceCalcCenter

Many financial decisions become easier to understand when the numbers are clearly laid out. A monthly payment may look affordable, but what is the total interest cost? A small daily expense may seem insignificant, but what does it cost over a year? How much could regular saving grow over time?

FinanceCalcCenter was created to help answer questions like these with simple calculators, transparent formulas, worked examples, and beginner-friendly explanations.

The goal is not to tell visitors what financial decision they should make. The goal is to give them useful numbers and explanations that can help them understand their options and make their own informed decisions.

How Our Calculators Work

FinanceCalcCenter calculators are built around standard financial formulas and simplified assumptions. Each calculator is designed to show estimated results based on the numbers entered by the user.

For example, a loan payment calculator may use the standard amortization formula to estimate monthly payments. A compound interest calculator may estimate growth based on starting balance, contribution amount, interest rate, compounding frequency, and time.

Calculator results are intended to be educational estimates. Actual financial results may differ because of taxes, fees, changing interest rates, lender rules, account terms, inflation, and other real-world factors.

How I Create and Review Content

Articles and guides on FinanceCalcCenter are written to explain financial basics in plain language. The content is organized around common user questions, such as how APR differs from APY, how compound interest works, or how much emergency savings may be useful.

When possible, guides include examples, tables, formulas, and links to related calculators. Content is reviewed for clarity, consistency, and usefulness before publication.

Existing pages may be updated when explanations can be improved, when calculator methodology changes, or when additional examples would make the topic easier to understand.

Editorial Principles

  • Clarity: We aim to explain financial topics in simple, direct language.
  • Transparency: Calculators should make assumptions and formulas easy to understand.
  • Practicality: Examples are designed around everyday financial situations.
  • Independence: FinanceCalcCenter does not provide personalized financial advice or recommend specific financial products.
  • Improvement: Pages may be revised when better explanations, examples, or calculator details are needed.

Accuracy and Limitations

FinanceCalcCenter aims to provide clear and useful estimates, but no online calculator can account for every personal situation. Financial products can include fees, taxes, promotional terms, variable rates, penalties, or rules that are not reflected in simplified calculators.

Users should review actual loan documents, account disclosures, mortgage estimates, credit card terms, and other official information before making financial decisions. For important decisions, consider speaking with a qualified financial, tax, legal, or mortgage professional.
