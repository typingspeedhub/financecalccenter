How Mortgage Payments Are Calculated

When people think about a mortgage payment, they often assume it is just the loan repayment. In reality, the amount you pay to your mortgage servicer may include several components. Understanding them helps you plan your budget, compare loan offers, and avoid surprises.

A typical U.S. mortgage payment can include four elements, often called PITI:

  • Principal — repayment of the loan balance
  • Interest — the cost of borrowing
  • Property taxes — local taxes on the property
  • Insurance — usually homeowners insurance, and sometimes mortgage insurance

Taxes and homeowners insurance are often collected by the lender through an escrow account, but sometimes you pay them separately. Additional expenses such as HOA dues and maintenance may not be included in the amount you send to the lender.

Principal: Repaying the Loan Amount

The principal is the amount borrowed. With a standard fully amortizing fixed-rate mortgage, every scheduled principal-and-interest payment reduces the balance over time.

Example: 20% down payment

Home price: $400,000
Down payment (20%): $80,000
Loan amount: $320,000

Early in the loan term, a greater share of the scheduled principal-and-interest payment typically goes toward interest. As the balance falls, the share going toward principal increases. The combined principal-and-interest payment generally stays constant on a standard fixed-rate loan, assuming no extra payments or changes to the loan.

Interest: The Cost of Borrowing

Interest is charged on the outstanding loan balance. For a standard fixed-rate mortgage with monthly payments, a common illustration uses the annual interest rate divided by 12 to obtain the monthly rate. Actual lender calculations and payment timing can vary by loan terms.

Example: 30-year fixed-rate mortgage

Loan amount: $320,000
Annual interest rate: 6%
Loan term: 30 years (360 months)
Monthly principal and interest: $1,918.56 (about $1,919)

Mortgage Payment Formula

For a conventional fully amortizing fixed-rate loan with equal monthly principal-and-interest payments, use:

M = P × [r(1 + r)n] / [(1 + r)n − 1]

  • M = monthly principal-and-interest payment
  • P = original loan principal
  • r = monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12, as a decimal)
  • n = total number of monthly payments (years × 12)

In this example: P = $320,000, r = 0.06 ÷ 12 = 0.005, and n = 30 × 12 = 360. Therefore M ≈ $1,918.56.

If the interest rate is 0%, the monthly principal-and-interest payment is simply P ÷ n. This formula does not include taxes, insurance, HOA fees, closing costs or adjustable-rate changes.

Property Taxes

Property taxes are assessed by state or local authorities and depend on the property and location. Many mortgage servicers collect an estimated monthly amount in escrow and pay the tax bill when due. Tax assessments can change.

Illustrative annual property tax: $4,800
Estimated monthly tax portion: $400.00 ($4,800 ÷ 12)

If taxes are not collected through escrow, you still need to budget for them and pay them separately.

Homeowners Insurance

Mortgage lenders generally require appropriate property insurance. Some insurance premiums are included in monthly escrow payments, while others are paid directly to the insurer.

Illustrative annual homeowners insurance: $1,200
Estimated monthly insurance portion: $100.00 ($1,200 ÷ 12)

Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI)

PMI typically applies to certain conventional mortgages when the down payment is less than 20%. It protects the lender, not the borrower. In the $400,000 home example above, the borrower makes a 20% down payment; PMI is therefore not included in the base example.

Other mortgage programs have different insurance requirements. Some conventional loans may offer lender-paid mortgage insurance or other pricing structures. The conditions for canceling borrower-paid PMI also depend on the loan.

Separate example: 5% down payment

Home price: $400,000
Down payment (5%): $20,000
Loan amount: $380,000
30-year fixed rate: 6%
Monthly principal and interest: $2,278.29
Illustrative property tax: $400.00
Illustrative homeowners insurance: $100.00
Illustrative monthly PMI: $150.00
Estimated total monthly payment: $2,928.29

The $150 PMI is a hypothetical illustration, not a quoted premium. Real PMI costs depend on the borrower, loan and insurer.

Costs That May Not Be Included

When comparing the payment with your household budget, also account for HOA or condominium dues, maintenance, repairs, utilities, upfront closing costs and possible changes in property taxes or insurance. HOA dues are often paid separately from the mortgage servicer.

Review the lender's Loan Estimate, especially the Projected Payments section, to see which costs are included in the estimated monthly payment.

Key Takeaways

  • A mortgage's principal-and-interest payment is not necessarily the entire monthly housing cost.
  • Taxes and homeowners insurance may be added through escrow or paid separately.
  • PMI usually applies to conventional mortgages with less than 20% down, depending on the loan structure.
  • A fixed rate generally keeps principal and interest stable, but taxes and insurance can change.
  • Compare the complete housing budget, not just the quoted loan payment.

Understanding these components can help you compare loan offers and plan more realistically. This article provides educational examples, not a lending quote or individualized financial advice.
